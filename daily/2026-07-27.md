## 2. Paramount, WBD put $110-billion merger on hold until court ruling or June 2027
- **Source:** bestmediainfo
- **Link:** https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/mediainfo-digital/paramount-wbd-put-110-billion-merger-on-hold-until-court-ruling-or-june-2027-12200716
- **Summary:** New Delhi: Paramount Skydance and Warner Bros. Discovery have agreed not to complete their proposed merger until five days after a federal court rules on the antitrust challenges to the transaction or June 1, 2027, whichever comes first. The agreement places one of Hollywood’s largest proposed consolidations on hold after a coalition of 12 US states and the Writers Guild of America filed separate lawsuits seeking to block the deal. The transaction values Warner Bros. Discovery’s equity at around $81 billion and has an enterprise value of about $110 billion, including debt. Under a joint stipulation filed in the US District Court for the Northern District of California, Paramount and WBD cannot close the transaction or take steps to integrate or consolidate their operations during the standstill period. The filing also cancels an August 3 hearing on the states’ request for a preliminary injunction. The WGA has withdrawn its preliminary injunction motion, although both sets of plaintiffs retain the right to seek interim relief later. The parties are required to submit proposed trial schedules by July 31. From temporary pause to full antitrust trial US District Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín had earlier issued a temporary restraining order after the states sought an immediate pause on the merger. The court subsequently extended that order until August 17 and asked the parties to agree on how the litigation should proceed. The new arrangement removes the immediate contest over a preliminary injunction and moves the dispute towards a trial on whether the merger itself violates US antitrust law. Paramount described the agreement as a favourable development because it provides “a direct path to a trial based on the evidence”. The company said it believed a trial was the fastest way to demonstrate that the transaction would benefit competition, consumers and creators, and maintained that the market definitions used by the plaintiffs did not reflect the current entertainment landscape. States see threat to films and cable television California Attorney General Rob Bonta is leading the coalition comprising Arizona, Colorado, Connecticut, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Nevada, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, Oregon and Washington. The states allege that the acquisition would eliminate competition between two of Hollywood’s five major film distributors and two of the five major owners of basic cable channels. According to their complaint, Paramount and WBD would together account for about 27% of wide-release theatrical film distribution in the US. They would also control more than 30% of films expected to be among the highest-grossing releases and approximately 27% of the market for licensing basic cable channels to distributors. The states argue that the consolidation could give the combined company greater bargaining power over cinemas and cable distributors, potentially leading to higher prices, less content and fewer choices for audiences. They also contend that cable and satellite distributors would lose the ability to use competition between Paramount and WBD to negotiate better carriage and licensing terms. Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser called the standstill a win for audiences, cinemas and cable distributors, while maintaining that the merger would substantially reduce competition. Writers fear fewer jobs and lower pay The Writers Guild of America West and Writers Guild of America East filed a separate antitrust case on July 14. The unions allege that the combined company would become the largest US buyer of original film and television programming, giving it greater power to reduce production, suppress writers’ compensation and limit employment opportunities. The WGA’s complaint focuses on competition for writing services across anticipated blockbuster films, episodic television and streaming series, and overall talent deals. The guild argues that the merger’s effects should therefore be examined not only from the perspective of audiences and distributors but also through its potential impact on those who create film and television programming. Delay could add around $650 million every quarter The standstill could significantly increase Paramount’s cost of acquiring WBD. Under the February merger agreement, WBD shareholders are entitled to receive $31 per share. For every day the transaction closes after September 30, 2026, the consideration increases by $0.00277778 per share, capped at an additional $0.25 for each 90-day period. At the company’s share count, the provision is estimated to add roughly $650 million to the purchase price for every quarter of delay, or about $7 million a day. A closing near June 1, 2027, could add approximately $1.7 billion to the transaction’s cost, although the final amount would depend on the closing date and eligible shares. The merger agreement has an initial outside date of March 4, 2027, which can automatically extend to June 4 if the remaining obstacles relate to regulatory approvals or government orders. If the transaction ultimately fails because regulatory clearances are not obtained or a final antitrust order blocks it, Paramount could be required to pay WBD a $7-billion regulatory termination fee, subject to the conditions set out in the merger agreement. Paramount points to global approvals The US Department of Justice has already cleared the merger, concluding that it was unlikely to harm competition in streaming, linear television or theatrical film development, production and distribution. The European Commission also cleared the transaction on July 22. Paramount said competition and foreign-investment authorities representing 65 jurisdictions had either approved the deal or decided not to challenge it. Paramount has argued that greater scale is required to compete with technology-led entertainment platforms and that the merger would support higher investment in content. The state attorneys general and the WGA, however, maintain that combining two major Hollywood studios would reduce independent sources of programming and concentrate too much control over film, television and creative employment. What the combined company would control The transaction would place Paramount Pictures and Warner Bros. Pictures under one owner, alongside streaming services Paramount+ and HBO Max. The combined group would also control a large portfolio of television networks and news properties, including CBS, CNN, Nickelodeon, MTV, Cartoon Network, HGTV and other WBD and Paramount channels. Paramount Skydance is led by David Ellison and was formed after Skydance completed its acquisition of Paramount in 2025.
