## 1. Vance Completes His Iran War Backflip: ‘My Job Is To Support The President’

- **Link:** https://www.huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/vance-iran-war-backflip-trump_uk_6b015407e4b0db04454ace32
- **Summary:** JD Vance is papering over his post-midterms criticism of the Iran war, arguing at a White House press conference on Friday that his task was merely to support Donald Trump’s decision to launch a conflict he’s said cost Republicans the election. The backtrack from the vice-president — who is widely expected to run for president in 2028 — comes amid reports that Trump was angry with Vance for breaking with the White House’s unflappable defence of the war and its catastrophic effects on the US economy. In his appearance alongside Trump and Secretary of State Marco Rubio, another potential 2028 presidential candidate, Vance largely echoed the standard Republican talking points about why attacking Iran was necessary. “What I’ve said is, the goal of keeping Iran from getting a nuclear weapon is exactly what we need to be doing,” Vance said during the news conference. “It’s the biggest state sponsor of terrorism. If they had a nuclear weapon, they would use it as leverage or, God forbid, they would attack and kill potentially hundreds of thousands of innocent people.” Voters sent a broad swathe of Republicans in Congress packing earlier this month in large part due to the Iran war’s impact on the cost of living. Vance, in a sober morning-after social media post, stated bluntly that “everyone knows” why his party faceplanted. “The reason we lost is the war,” he wrote in a November 4 post on X. And he challenged the war’s defenders to explain why it was a “worthwhile cost” for Republicans to lose the Senate. But any glimmer of defiance from Vance had dulled by Friday afternoon, when the vice president told reporters he agreed with Trump’s rationale for attacking Iran and kickstarting a regional conflict. Trump has repeatedly claimed he went to war in Iran without congressional authorisation to prevent the regime from getting a nuclear weapon, though it’s unclear how successful those aims have been nearly a year into the conflict. The president told reporters he would do it all over again even if it meant another shellacking at the polls. “Absolutely, the answer is yes, because we cannot allow Iran to have a nuclear weapon,” Trump said. The president has also denied reports that he’d been upset with Vance for making Iran the culprit for the Republicans’ midterms loss, saying he thought the vice president’s statement was “fine.” Trump told reporters earlier this month that “a lot of people didn’t like” Vance’s social media commentary. Vance, who was reportedly sceptical of plans to strike Iran before the war began in March, distanced himself completely on Friday from any decision-making authority on the conflict. “I think the president is the one who makes the decision, and the president has said he would do it again,” the vice president argued. “My view is that my goal, and my job, is to support the president of the United States.” Trump, for his part, has acknowledged that the Iran war was a “a part” of why Republicans lost so tremendously in the midterms, which saw Democrats win control of the House and Senate as well as the majority of state governorships in an election pundits have widely framed as a rejection of the president. But he’s argued separately that his party would not have gotten pummelled so badly had he been on the ballot.
